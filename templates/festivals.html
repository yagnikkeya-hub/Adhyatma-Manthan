Regional Traditions

One Land. Many Ways to Celebrate.

India is a diverse country. Across different states, the same festivals are celebrated with local songs, dances, food, and beautiful customs. Click below to see how different regions celebrate.

WESTERN INDIA • GUJARAT

Garba: Dancing Together in Circles

In Gujarat, Navratri is a vibrant nine-night festival where young and old put on colorful traditional clothes and dance Garba and Dandiya together around a softly glowing lamp.

Why it matters: Dancing in a circle reminds us that while our daily lives keep changing, goodness and truth stay steady in the center. The day after Diwali is celebrated as Bestu Varas (New Year), where people forgive past fights and make a fresh start.
KEY TRADITIONS
  • Fasting on light fruits and milk to give the body rest;
  • Singing devotional songs (Aartis) together at midnight;
  • Visiting friends and family on New Year's day with sweets.
EASTERN INDIA • BENGAL

Welcoming Maa Durga Like a Beloved Daughter

In Bengal, Durga Puja is celebrated with immense joy. Beautiful clay idols are made in every locality, and Maa Durga is welcomed with the energetic beat of dhak drums like a loving mother and daughter visiting home.

Why it matters: On Diwali night, people celebrate Kali Puja to overcome fear, bad habits, and anger. It reminds us that inner courage is the greatest protection we have.
KEY TRADITIONS
  • Making eco-friendly clay idols from river soil;
  • Traditional Dhunuchi dances with burning incense;
  • Sharing sweets like Rasgulla and Sandesh with neighbors.
MAHARASHTRA • JANMAASHTAMI

Dahi Handi: Great Teamwork and Balance

On the day after Krishna Janmashtami, groups of youth called 'Govindas' work together to build tall human pyramids to reach and break an earthen pot filled with curd hanging high up.

Why it matters: Just like child Krishna shared butter with all his cowherd friends, Dahi Handi teaches us that real success comes from trusting each other and helping teammates balance, rather than trying to win alone.
KEY TRADITIONS
  • Practice and safety gear so everyone stays safe;
  • Crowds cheering and singing songs of Lord Krishna;
  • Sharing Prasad made from beaten rice, curd, and fruits.
NORTH INDIA • DIWALI & DUSSEHRA

Lighting Ayodhya & Dussehra Plays

Across northern India, families watch Ramlila plays that tell the story of the Ramayana. On Dussehra evening, effigies of Ravana are burned to remind everyone that pride always falls.

Why it matters: During Diwali, millions of lamps are lit along river ghats in Ayodhya and Varanasi. Families pray together for good health, wisdom, and peace in their homes.
KEY TRADITIONS
  • Evening family prayers with Lord Ganesha and Goddess Lakshmi;
  • Decorating doorways with warm lights and flower garlands;
  • Brothers and sisters celebrating their bond on Bhai Dooj.
SOUTH INDIA • DEEPAVALI

Pre-Dawn Oil Bath & Healthy Living

In Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana, the main Diwali celebration starts before sunrise. Families wake up early, apply warm sesame oil infused with herbs, and take a refreshing bath.

Why it matters: Taking an early morning bath represents washing away tiredness and negative feelings, starting the day with pure energy and good health.
KEY TRADITIONS
  • Eating healthy herbal ginger paste (Marundhu) to help digestion;
  • Greeting friends and neighbors early in the morning in new clothes;
  • Arranging steps of traditional dolls (Golu) during Navratri.
KERALA • VIJAYADASHAMI

Vidyarambham: Learning Your First Words

In Kerala, the final day of Navratri (Vijayadashami) is celebrated as Vidyarambham—a special day when little children are taught how to write their first letters.

Why it matters: An elder or teacher gently guides the child's index finger to write letters in a plate of clean raw rice or sand. It teaches that education is a sacred journey that deserves our utmost respect.
KEY TRADITIONS
  • Placing textbooks and musical instruments before Goddess Saraswati;
  • Starting new music, dance, or computer classes on this day;
  • Showing gratitude to teachers, mentors, and parents.

Kindness • Helping Others

Let Celebration Become Helping (Seva)

True happiness grows when we share our festive joy with others who need it. Here are simple, realistic ways you can help without spending a lot of money.

🍲

Sharing Meals (Annadaan)

Pack a simple homemade meal or fresh fruits and hand them politely to homeless people, cleaning staff, or helpers around your area.

📚

Gifting Books & Stationery

Gift pencils, notebooks, school bags, or storybooks to children of domestic helpers or daily-wage workers who are studying hard.

🪔

Supporting Local Potters

Buy your diyas and handmade decorations directly from roadside artisans and potters without bargaining down their hard work.

🐾

Caring for Animals & Birds

Keep a shallow bowl of clean water on your balcony for birds, and feed stray animals with healthy food instead of ignoring them.

🧹

Keeping Surroundings Clean

Help clean your street or neighborhood park after celebrations. Avoid throwing plastic waste or loud crackers that scare pets and elders.

🤍

Spending Time with Elders

Spend half an hour talking to elderly grandparents or neighbors who live alone. Listening to them with patience is a great act of kindness.
